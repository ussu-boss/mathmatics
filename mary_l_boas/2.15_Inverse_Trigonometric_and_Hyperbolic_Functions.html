2.15 Inverse Trigonometric and Hyperbolic Functions

Boas, Chapter 2, Section 15 · solving $\cos z=2$, $\sin z=5/3$, $\tan z=2i$…: the $u=e^{iz}$ method, log formulas for every inverse function, all solutions, and the values $\tan z$ can never take.

Goals
  • Solve $f(z)=w$ for $f=\sin,\cos,\tan,\sinh,\cosh,\tanh$, finding all solutions.
  • Derive the logarithm formulas for $\arcsin,\arccos,\arctan,\sinh^{-1},\cosh^{-1},\tanh^{-1}$.
  • Know the structure of the solution set: a lattice of points repeating with the period.
  • Connect to calculus: why integral tables give $\sinh^{-1}\frac xa$ or $\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2})$.
  • Solve all 18 of Boas's Section 15 problems.

1. Definition

We have defined $w=\cos z=\frac{e^{iz}+e^{-iz}}{2}$ (15.1). The inverse cosine is defined by (15.2):

$$z=\arccos w\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w=\cos z,$$

and similarly for the other inverse trigonometric and hyperbolic functions. For real numbers $|\cos x|\le1$, so "$\arccos2$" has no real answer. For complex $z$, however, $\cos z$ takes every complex value, so $\arccos2$ exists. In fact it has infinitely many values.

H.K. Dass §20.30–20.31Inverse hyperbolic functions: all six log formulas, derived

Definition (§20.30). If $\sin\theta=z$ then $\theta=\sin^{-1}z$, the inverse sine of $z$. In the same way, $\cosh\theta=z$ means $\theta=\cosh^{-1}z$, and so on. Each inverse is found by solving a quadratic in $e^y$.

(1) $\sinh^{-1}x=\log\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+1}\right)$. Let $x=\sinh y=\frac{e^y-e^{-y}}2$. Then $e^{2y}-2xe^y-1=0$, so $e^y=x\pm\sqrt{x^2+1}$. For real $x$, $x-\sqrt{x^2+1}\lt0$ but $e^y\gt0$, so only the $+$ sign survives.

(2) $\cosh^{-1}x=\log\left(x+\sqrt{x^2-1}\right)$ ($x\ge1$). Here $e^{2y}-2xe^y+1=0$, so $e^y=x\pm\sqrt{x^2-1}$. Both roots are positive and their product is $1$, so they give $\pm y$ ($\cosh$ is even). The principal value takes the $+$ sign, so $y\ge0$.

(3) $\tanh^{-1}x=\frac12\log\frac{1+x}{1-x}$ ($|x|\lt1$). From $x=\frac{e^y-e^{-y}}{e^y+e^{-y}}$, componendo–dividendo gives $\frac{1+x}{1-x}=e^{2y}$. Similarly $\coth^{-1}x=\frac12\log\frac{x+1}{x-1}$ ($|x|\gt1$).

(4) $\operatorname{sech}^{-1}x=\log\dfrac{1+\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x}$ ($0\lt x\le1$). From $x=\frac{2}{e^y+e^{-y}}$: $xe^{2y}-2e^y+x=0$, so $e^y=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x}$. Take the $+$ sign for the principal value. Similarly $\operatorname{cosech}^{-1}x=\log\dfrac{1+\sqrt{1+x^2}}{x}$ ($x\gt0$).

For complex arguments the same formulas hold, with $\log$ and $\sqrt{\ }$ taken as complex (multivalued) functions. That is exactly the "Log formulas" table above.

Worked example (Dass §20.31) — if $\cos^{-1}(x+iy)=\alpha+i\beta$, then $\cos^2\alpha$ and $\cosh^2\beta$ are the roots of $\lambda^2-(x^2+y^2+1)\lambda+x^2=0$
  1. $x+iy=\cos(\alpha+i\beta)=\cos\alpha\cosh\beta-i\sin\alpha\sinh\beta$, so $x=\cos\alpha\cosh\beta$ and $y=-\sin\alpha\sinh\beta$.
  2. $x^2+y^2+1=\cos^2\alpha\cosh^2\beta+(1-\cos^2\alpha)(\cosh^2\beta-1)+1=\cos^2\alpha+\cosh^2\beta$. This is the sum of the claimed roots.
  3. Their product is $\cos^2\alpha\cosh^2\beta=x^2$. So the quadratic follows from Vieta. $\blacksquare$

Why this matters for CSIR-NET / GATE: finding "the equation whose roots are …" by sum and product avoids solving for $\alpha,\beta$ at all.

Worked example (Dass §20.31) — separate $\cos^{-1}\frac{3i}{4}$
  1. Let $\cos^{-1}\frac{3i}4=x+iy$. Then $\cos x\cosh y=0$ and $-\sin x\sinh y=\frac34$.
  2. $\cosh y\ne0$, so $\cos x=0$: take $x=\frac\pi2$ (principal). Then $\sinh y=-\frac34$.
  3. $y=\sinh^{-1}\left(-\frac34\right)=\log\left(-\frac34+\frac54\right)=\log\frac12=-\log2$.
  4. So $\cos^{-1}\frac{3i}4=\frac\pi2-i\log2$. Check: $\cos\left(\frac\pi2-i\log2\right)=\sin(i\log2)=i\sinh(\log2)=\frac{3i}4$. ✓

Why this matters for CSIR-NET / GATE: inverse trig of a purely imaginary number always has real part $\frac\pi2$ (for $\cos^{-1}$) or $0$ (for $\sin^{-1}$). Spotting this halves the work.

Worked example (Dass §20.32) — $\cosh^{-1}\sqrt{1+x^2}=\sinh^{-1}x=\tanh^{-1}\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}$ ($x\gt0$)
  1. Let $\cosh y=\sqrt{1+x^2}$. Then $\sinh^2y=\cosh^2y-1=x^2$, so $\sinh y=x$ and $y=\sinh^{-1}x$.
  2. Dividing, $\tanh y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}$, so $y=\tanh^{-1}\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}$. $\blacksquare$

Why this matters for CSIR-NET / GATE: in relativity this says that $\gamma=\cosh\phi$, $\gamma\beta=\sinh\phi$ and $\beta=\tanh\phi$ all describe the same rapidity $\phi$.

H.K. Dass §20.32Other inverse functions: separating $\sin^{-1}$, $\tan^{-1}$ of $a+ib$

$\tan^{-1}(a+ib)$. Let $\tan(x+iy)=a+ib$, so that $\tan(x-iy)=a-ib$. Adding and subtracting the angles (as in 2.11):

$$\tan2x=\frac{2a}{1-a^2-b^2},\qquad\tanh2y=\frac{2b}{1+a^2+b^2},$$ $$\tan^{-1}(a+ib)=\frac12\tan^{-1}\frac{2a}{1-a^2-b^2}+\frac i2\tanh^{-1}\frac{2b}{1+a^2+b^2}.$$

Special case $a+ib=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$: then $1-a^2-b^2=0$, so $\tan2x=\infty$ and $x=\frac{n\pi}2+\frac\pi4$, while $\tanh2y=\sin\theta$ gives $y=\frac12\log\tan\left(\frac\pi4+\frac\theta2\right)$.

$\sin^{-1}(\alpha+i\beta)$. Let $\sin(x+iy)=\alpha+i\beta$, so $\alpha=\sin x\cosh y$ and $\beta=\cos x\sinh y$.

  • Eliminate $y$ with $\cosh^2y-\sinh^2y=1$: $\sin^4x-(\alpha^2+\beta^2+1)\sin^2x+\alpha^2=0$, a quadratic in $\sin^2x$.
  • Eliminate $x$ with $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$: $\sinh^4y-(\alpha^2+\beta^2-1)\sinh^2y-\beta^2=0$, a quadratic in $\sinh^2y$.
$$\sin^2x=\tfrac12\left[(\alpha^2+\beta^2+1)-\sqrt{(\alpha^2+\beta^2+1)^2-4\alpha^2}\right],\qquad\sinh^2y=\tfrac12\left[(\alpha^2+\beta^2-1)+\sqrt{(\alpha^2+\beta^2-1)^2+4\beta^2}\right].$$

Dass writes $\pm$ for both roots. For the principal value, take the smaller root for $\sin^2x$ (it must be $\le1$) and the positive root for $\sinh^2y$ (it must be $\ge0$). Then give $x$ the sign of $\alpha$ and $y$ the sign of $\beta$. $\cos^{-1}w=\frac\pi2-\sin^{-1}w$.

Worked example (Dass §20.32) — $\tan^{-1}\left(i\,\dfrac{x-a}{x+a}\right)=\dfrac i2\log\dfrac xa$ ($x,a\gt0$)
  1. Let the left side be $u+iv$. Then $\tan(u+iv)=i\frac{x-a}{x+a}$ and $\tan(u-iv)=-i\frac{x-a}{x+a}$.
  2. $\tan2u=\frac{\text{sum}}{1-\text{product}}=0$, so $u=0$.
  3. $\tan(iv)=i\tanh v=i\frac{x-a}{x+a}$, so $\tanh v=\frac{x-a}{x+a}$. Componendo–dividendo gives $e^{2v}=\frac xa$, i.e. $v=\frac12\log\frac xa$. $\blacksquare$

Why this matters for CSIR-NET / GATE: an inverse tangent of a purely imaginary number is purely imaginary, and equals $i\tanh^{-1}$. That converts it into a logarithm immediately.

2. The method: put $u=e^{iz}$ and get a quadratic

Boas Example 1 — find $\arccos2$
  1. We want $z$ with $\cos z=2$, i.e. $\dfrac{e^{iz}+e^{-iz}}{2}=2$.
  2. Let $u=e^{iz}$, so $e^{-iz}=u^{-1}$: $\ \dfrac{u+u^{-1}}{2}=2$.
  3. Multiply by $2u$: $u^2+1=4u$, i.e. $u^2-4u+1=0$.
  4. Quadratic formula: $u=\dfrac{4\pm\sqrt{16-4}}{2}=2\pm\sqrt3$. So $e^{iz}=2\pm\sqrt3$.
  5. Take logs: $iz=\ln(2\pm\sqrt3)=\operatorname{Ln}(2\pm\sqrt3)+2n\pi i$ (both numbers are positive).
  6. $z=2n\pi-i\operatorname{Ln}(2\pm\sqrt3)=2n\pi\pm i\operatorname{Ln}(2+\sqrt3)$, since $\operatorname{Ln}(2-\sqrt3)=-\operatorname{Ln}(2+\sqrt3)$ (because $(2-\sqrt3)(2+\sqrt3)=1$).
  7. Numerically: $\arccos2=2n\pi\pm1.317i$.

Check (as Boas does): with $e^{iz}=2\pm\sqrt3$, $\ e^{-iz}=\frac{1}{2\pm\sqrt3}=2\mp\sqrt3$, so $\cos z=\frac{(2\pm\sqrt3)+(2\mp\sqrt3)}{2}=\frac42=2$ ✔.

The recipe for any of the six
  1. Write $f(z)=w$ using exponentials ($e^{iz}$ for trig, $e^{z}$ for hyperbolic).
  2. Substitute $u=e^{iz}$ (or $u=e^z$) and multiply through to get a quadratic in $u$ (or a linear equation, for tan/tanh).
  3. Solve for $u$ (two values, using a complex square root if needed, Section 2.5D).
  4. Take $\ln$ of each $u$, keeping all branches ($+2n\pi i$).
  5. Solve for $z$ and simplify. Check by substituting back.

A second method: split into real and imaginary parts

For $\sin z=5/3$ (Boas Problem 8): $\sin x\cosh y=\frac53$ and $\cos x\sinh y=0$.

  • If $\sinh y=0$, then $y=0$ and $\sin x=\frac53\gt1$: impossible.
  • So $\cos x=0$, giving $x=\frac\pi2+k\pi$. Then $\sin x=\pm1$, and $\cosh y=\pm\frac53$. Since $\cosh y\ge1$, only $\sin x=+1$ works: $x=\frac\pi2+2n\pi$ and $\cosh y=\frac53$, so $y=\pm\operatorname{Ln}\left(\frac53+\frac43\right)=\pm\operatorname{Ln}3$.
  • $\arcsin\frac53=\frac\pi2+2n\pi\pm i\operatorname{Ln}3$.

Show derivations

arcsin: $\frac{u-u^{-1}}{2i}=z$ gives $u^2-2izu-1=0$, so $u=iz\pm\sqrt{1-z^2}$, and $w=-i\ln u$.

arccos: $u^2-2zu+1=0$ gives $u=z\pm\sqrt{z^2-1}$, so $w=-i\ln u$. Since the two values of $u$ multiply to 1, $-i\ln(z+\sqrt{\ })=i\ln(z-\sqrt{\ })$, and Boas writes $i\ln(z\pm\sqrt{z^2-1})$.

arctan: $\frac{u-u^{-1}}{i(u+u^{-1})}=z$ gives $u^2-1=iz(u^2+1)$, so $u^2=\frac{1+iz}{1-iz}$ and $w=\frac1{2i}\ln u^2$.

sinh⁻¹: with $u=e^w$: $\frac{u-u^{-1}}2=z$, so $u^2-2zu-1=0$ and $u=z\pm\sqrt{z^2+1}$.

cosh⁻¹: $u^2-2zu+1=0$, so $u=z\pm\sqrt{z^2-1}$. The two roots are reciprocals, which gives the $\pm\ln$ form (Problem 17.21).

tanh⁻¹: $\frac{u-u^{-1}}{u+u^{-1}}=z$, so $u^2=\frac{1+z}{1-z}$.

Why "all solutions" look like that: $\sin(\pi-z)=\sin z$ and $\sin$ has period $2\pi$. $\cos$ is even with period $2\pi$. $\tan$ has period $\pi$. For the hyperbolic functions, replace $\pi$ by $\pi i$.

4. Boas Example 2 — two forms of the same integral

Integral tables give $\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}}$ either as $\sinh^{-1}\frac xa$ or as $\ln\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}\right)$ (15.3–15.4). How are these related?

  1. Put $z=\sinh^{-1}\frac xa$, i.e. $\frac xa=\sinh z=\frac{e^z-e^{-z}}{2}$ (15.5).
  2. With $u=e^z$: $u-\frac1u=\frac{2x}{a}$, i.e. $au^2-2xu-a=0$, so $u=\dfrac{x\pm\sqrt{x^2+a^2}}{a}$ (15.6).
  3. For real $z$, $e^z>0$, so take the $+$ sign. Then $z=\ln\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}\right)-\ln a$ (15.7).
  4. The two answers differ only by the constant $\ln a$, which is absorbed into the constant of integration ✔.
Hidden complex logs in real calculus

$\arctan x=\frac{1}{2i}\ln\frac{1+ix}{1-ix}$. So the real integral $\int\frac{dx}{1+x^2}$ is secretly a complex logarithm. Putting $x=1$: $\frac\pi4=\frac1{2i}\ln\frac{1+i}{1-i}=\frac1{2i}\ln i=\frac1{2i}\cdot\frac{i\pi}{2}$ ✔.

5. Values that are never taken (Boas Problems 17, 18)

$\tan z\ne\pm i$ and $\tanh z\ne\pm1$

Suppose $\tan z=i$: $\ \dfrac{e^{iz}-e^{-iz}}{i(e^{iz}+e^{-iz})}=i$, so $e^{iz}-e^{-iz}=-(e^{iz}+e^{-iz})$, i.e. $2e^{iz}=0$. That is impossible, since $e^{w}\ne0$. Similarly $\tan z=-i$ needs $e^{-iz}=0$.

For $\tanh z=1$: $e^z-e^{-z}=e^z+e^{-z}$, so $e^{-z}=0$, which is impossible. In the formula $\tanh^{-1}z=\frac12\ln\frac{1+z}{1-z}$, $z=\pm1$ makes the argument of $\ln$ infinite or zero.

By contrast, $\sin z$, $\cos z$, $\sinh z$, $\cosh z$ take every complex value: their quadratics always have nonzero roots, since the product of the roots is $\mp1$.

7. More worked examples

Solve $\sin z=i$
  1. $z=-i\ln\left(i\cdot i\pm\sqrt{1-i^2}\right)=-i\ln(-1\pm\sqrt2)$.
  2. $+$: $\ln(\sqrt2-1)=-\operatorname{Ln}(1+\sqrt2)+2n\pi i$, so $z=2n\pi+i\operatorname{Ln}(1+\sqrt2)$.
  3. $-$: $\ln(-1-\sqrt2)=\operatorname{Ln}(1+\sqrt2)+i(\pi+2n\pi)$, so $z=(2n+1)\pi-i\operatorname{Ln}(1+\sqrt2)$.
  4. Check: $\sin(i\cdot0.8814)=i\sinh(0.8814)=i\cdot1=i$ ✔.
Solve $\cos z=-3$
  1. Try $z=\pi+iy$: $\cos(\pi+iy)=-\cosh y=-3$, so $y=\pm\cosh^{-1}3=\pm\operatorname{Ln}(3+2\sqrt2)$.
  2. All solutions: $z=(2n+1)\pi\pm i\operatorname{Ln}(3+2\sqrt2)=(2n+1)\pi\pm1.763i$.
Solve $\tanh z=\frac i{\sqrt3}$ using $\tanh(iy)=i\tan y$

$i\tan y=\frac{i}{\sqrt3}$, so $y=\frac\pi6+n\pi$ and $z=i\left(\frac\pi6+n\pi\right)$. (Check with the formula: $\frac12\ln\frac{1+i/\sqrt3}{1-i/\sqrt3}=\frac12\ln e^{i\pi/3}=\frac{i\pi}{6}$ ✔.)

10. Going deeper: branch points, derivatives, and the integral table

Where the inverses are singular

Every inverse function here is a logarithm of an algebraic expression. It becomes multivalued, or blows up, where the log's argument is $0$ or $\infty$, or where a square root inside it vanishes:

FunctionLog formFinite branch points
$\arcsin z$$-i\ln\!\left(iz+\sqrt{1-z^2}\right)$$z=\pm1$
$\arctan z$$\frac1{2i}\ln\frac{1+iz}{1-iz}$$z=\pm i$ (undefined there: $\tan z\ne\pm i$)
$\sinh^{-1}z$$\ln\!\left(z+\sqrt{z^2+1}\right)$$z=\pm i$
$\cosh^{-1}z$$\ln\!\left(z+\sqrt{z^2-1}\right)$$z=\pm1$
$\tanh^{-1}z$$\frac12\ln\frac{1+z}{1-z}$$z=\pm1$ (undefined there)

Derivatives straight from the log forms

Example: $\frac{d}{dz}\ln\!\left(z+\sqrt{z^2+1}\right)=\frac{1+\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+1}}}{z+\sqrt{z^2+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{z^2+1}}$. In the same way:

$$\frac{d}{dz}\arcsin z=\frac1{\sqrt{1-z^2}},\quad\frac{d}{dz}\arccos z=-\frac1{\sqrt{1-z^2}},\quad\frac{d}{dz}\arctan z=\frac1{1+z^2},\quad\frac{d}{dz}\tanh^{-1}z=\frac1{1-z^2}.$$

The inverse-function form and the log form differ only by a constant (for example $\ln a$), which is absorbed into $C$. This is Boas Example 2 again.

  • $\cosh^{-1}2=\ln(2+\sqrt3)\approx1.317$ and $\sinh^{-1}1=\ln(1+\sqrt2)\approx0.881$.

14. Summary

  • Method: $u=e^{iz}$ (or $e^z$) gives a quadratic in $u$; then $z$ comes from the logs of its roots.
  • $\arccos z=i\ln(z\pm\sqrt{z^2-1})$, $\arcsin z=-i\ln(iz\pm\sqrt{1-z^2})$, $\arctan z=\frac1{2i}\ln\frac{1+iz}{1-iz}$, and the hyperbolic ones are similar.
  • Solutions repeat: $\pm z_0+2n\pi$ (cos), $\{z_0,\pi-z_0\}+2n\pi$ (sin), $z_0+n\pi$ (tan), with $\pi\to\pi i$ for the hyperbolic functions.
  • $\tan z\ne\pm i$ and $\tanh z\ne\pm1$. The others take every value.
