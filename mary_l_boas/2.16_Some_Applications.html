2.16 Some Applications

Boas, Chapter 2, Section 16 · motion of a particle, a.c. circuits and complex impedance, adding many light waves (optics), and simple harmonic motion: complex numbers doing real physics.

Goals
  • Describe a motion $z(t)$: path, speed $|\dot z|$, and acceleration $|\ddot z|$, especially circular motion.
  • Use complex impedance $Z=R+i\left(\omega L-\frac1{\omega C}\right)$ to solve a.c. circuits like d.c. ones: series, parallel, resonance, phase.
  • Sum phase-shifted waves with a geometric series and get the $N$-slit interference formula.
  • Represent oscillations as $\operatorname{Re}$ or $\operatorname{Im}$ of $y_0e^{i\omega t}$.
  • Solve all 13 of Boas's Section 16 problems.

1. Motion of a particle

A position $z(t)=x(t)+iy(t)$ describes a path in the plane. As in Section 2.5F, the speed is $v=|dz/dt|$ and the acceleration magnitude is $a=|d^2z/dt^2|$.

Boas example — $z=1+3e^{2it}$
  1. $|z-1|=|3e^{2it}|=3$. Recall that $|z-1|$ is the distance from $z$ to the point $1$, so the particle moves on the circle of radius 3 centred at $(1,0)$ (16.1).
  2. $\dfrac{dz}{dt}=6ie^{2it}$, so $v=6$: constant speed.
  3. $\dfrac{d^2z}{dt^2}=-12e^{2it}=-4(z-1)$: magnitude 12, pointing to the centre ($=v^2/r=36/3$ ✔).
Multiplying by $i$ rotates by $90^\circ$ (Boas Problem 1)

$iz=e^{i\pi/2}z$ has the same length as $z$ and an angle larger by $\pi/2$. For uniform circular motion $z=ae^{i\omega t}$:

$$\dot z=i\omega z\ \ (\perp z,\ \text{speed }v=a\omega),\qquad\ddot z=(i\omega)^2z=-\omega^2z\ \ (\text{towards the centre},\ |\ddot z|=a\omega^2=\frac{v^2}{a}).$$

So the centripetal acceleration $v^2/a$ comes out in one line.

2. Electricity: a.c. circuits and complex impedance

For a resistance $R$, inductance $L$ and capacitance $C$:

$$V_R=IR\ \ (16.2),\qquad V_L=L\frac{dI}{dt}\ \ (16.3),\qquad\frac{dV_C}{dt}=\frac IC\ \ (16.4).$$

With a real current $I=I_0\sin\omega t$ (16.5) in the series circuit of Fig. 16.1, the voltages are $V_R=RI_0\sin\omega t$, $V_L=\omega LI_0\cos\omega t$, $V_C=-\frac{1}{\omega C}I_0\cos\omega t$ (16.6–16.8). Their sum (16.9) is awkward to work with.

The complex method

Write $I=I_0e^{i\omega t}$ (16.10), with the understanding that the physical current is $\operatorname{Im}I$ and the amplitude is $|I|=I_0$. Since $\frac{d}{dt}e^{i\omega t}=i\omega e^{i\omega t}$ and $\int e^{i\omega t}dt=\frac{e^{i\omega t}}{i\omega}$:

$$V_R=RI,\qquad V_L=i\omega L\,I,\qquad V_C=\frac{1}{i\omega C}I\qquad(16.11\text{–}16.13)$$ $$V=V_R+V_L+V_C=\left[R+i\left(\omega L-\frac{1}{\omega C}\right)\right]I=ZI\qquad(16.14\text{–}16.16)$$
Complex impedance $$Z=R+i\left(\omega L-\frac1{\omega C}\right)\qquad(16.15)$$

$V=ZI$ looks like Ohm's law: $Z$ plays the role of $R$. Its parts:

  • $|Z|=\sqrt{R^2+(\omega L-1/\omega C)^2}$ gives the ratio of the amplitudes: $V_0=|Z|I_0$.
  • $\arg Z=\phi$ is the phase by which the voltage leads the current.
  • Element impedances: $Z_R=R$, $Z_L=i\omega L$ ($+90^\circ$: voltage leads), $Z_C=\frac{1}{i\omega C}=-\frac{i}{\omega C}$ ($-90^\circ$: voltage lags).
Combining impedances (same rules as resistors) $$\text{series: }Z=Z_1+Z_2,\qquad\text{parallel: }\frac1Z=\frac1{Z_1}+\frac1{Z_2},\ \text{i.e. }Z=\frac{Z_1Z_2}{Z_1+Z_2}.$$

Resonance means $Z$ is real (current and voltage in phase). For the series RLC circuit, $\omega L=\frac1{\omega C}$, so $\omega_0=\frac1{\sqrt{LC}}$, and there $|Z|$ is smallest and the current largest.

3. Optics: adding $n$ waves with equal phase steps

Waves $\sin\omega t$, $\sin(\omega t+\delta)$, $\sin(\omega t+2\delta),\dots$ are the imaginary parts of $e^{i\omega t},\ e^{i(\omega t+\delta)},\dots$ (16.17). This is a geometric progression with first term $e^{i\omega t}$ and ratio $e^{i\delta}$. The sum of $n$ terms is (16.18)

$$\frac{e^{i\omega t}(1-e^{in\delta})}{1-e^{i\delta}} .$$

Factor out half-angles, as in Section 2.9: $1-e^{i\delta}=e^{i\delta/2}(e^{-i\delta/2}-e^{i\delta/2})=-2ie^{i\delta/2}\sin\frac\delta2$ (16.19), and likewise for $1-e^{in\delta}$. Then (16.20):

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{i(\omega t+k\delta)}=e^{i[\omega t+(n-1)\delta/2]}\,\frac{\sin(n\delta/2)}{\sin(\delta/2)}.$$

Taking the imaginary part, the total wave is

$$\boxed{\sin\left(\omega t+\frac{n-1}{2}\delta\right)\frac{\sin(n\delta/2)}{\sin(\delta/2)}}$$

a single wave with amplitude $\frac{\sin(n\delta/2)}{\sin(\delta/2)}$. The intensity (amplitude squared) $\frac{\sin^2(n\delta/2)}{\sin^2(\delta/2)}$ is the famous $N$-slit diffraction grating pattern: sharp principal maxima of height $n^2$ at $\delta=2\pi k$, with $n-2$ small subsidiary maxima in between.

4. Simple harmonic motion

A mass $m$ on a spring (Fig. 16.3) with displacement $y$ feels the force $-ky$. Newton's law gives (16.21)

$$m\frac{d^2y}{dt^2}=-ky\qquad\text{or}\qquad\frac{d^2y}{dt^2}=-\omega^2y,\quad\omega^2=\frac km .$$

We want a function whose second derivative is a constant times itself. $e^{i\omega t}$, $e^{-i\omega t}$, $\cos\omega t$ and $\sin\omega t$ all work (Problem 13: e.g. $\frac{d^2}{dt^2}e^{i\omega t}=(i\omega)^2e^{i\omega t}=-\omega^2e^{i\omega t}$). We write (16.22)

$$y=y_0e^{i\omega t},$$

understanding that the physical displacement is the real (or imaginary) part. If $y_0=Ae^{i\phi}$ is complex, then $\operatorname{Re}y=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)$. The modulus is the amplitude and the angle is the phase. $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}$ is the angular frequency.

5. Boas Problems, Section 16 — solutions

Show solutions 1–5 (motion)

1. If $z=re^{i\theta}$, then $iz=re^{i(\theta+\pi/2)}$: the same length, turned by $90^\circ$. For $z=ae^{i\omega t}$: $\dot z=i\omega z$, which is perpendicular to the radius, with speed $|\dot z|=a\omega=v$. Next, $\ddot z=i\omega\dot z=-\omega^2z$: turned another $90^\circ$, so it points from $z$ to the centre, with magnitude $a\omega^2=\frac{(a\omega)^2}{a}=\frac{v^2}a$.

2. $z=5e^{i\omega t}$: circle of radius 5 about the origin, counter-clockwise if $\omega>0$. Speed $5\omega$, acceleration $5\omega^2$ (towards the centre).

3. $z=(1+i)e^{it}=\sqrt2e^{i(t+\pi/4)}$: circle of radius $\sqrt2$ starting at $1+i$, with speed $\sqrt2$ and acceleration $\sqrt2$.

4. $z=(1+i)t-(2+i)(1-t)=(3+2i)t-(2+i)$. At $t=0$ it is at $-2-i$, and at $t=1$ at $1+i$: a straight line through these points, traversed with constant velocity $\dot z=3+2i$. Speed $\sqrt{13}$, acceleration $0$.

5. $z=z_1t+z_2(1-t)=z_2+(z_1-z_2)t$: a straight line through $z_2$ ($t=0$) and $z_1$ ($t=1$). Speed $|z_1-z_2|$ (constant), acceleration $0$. (At $t=\frac12$ it is at the midpoint.)

Show solution 8 — Fig. 16.2: $(R+L)\parallel C$

$Z_1=R+i\omega L$ and $Z_2=\frac1{i\omega C}$, so

$$\frac1Z=\frac{1}{R+i\omega L}+i\omega C\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z=\frac{R+i\omega L}{1-\omega^2LC+i\omega RC}.$$

Resonance: $Z$ is real iff $1/Z$ is real. $\operatorname{Im}\frac1Z=-\frac{\omega L}{R^2+\omega^2L^2}+\omega C=0$ gives $C(R^2+\omega^2L^2)=L$, so

$$\omega=\sqrt{\frac1{LC}-\frac{R^2}{L^2}}\qquad(\text{needs }R^2\lt L/C).$$

At resonance $Z=\frac{R^2+\omega^2L^2}{R}=\frac{L}{RC}$.

Show solution 9 — series RLC (Fig. 16.1)

(a) $\arg Z=45^\circ$ means $\omega L-\frac1{\omega C}=R$, i.e. $LC\omega^2-RC\omega-1=0$. The positive root is

$$\omega=\frac{RC+\sqrt{R^2C^2+4LC}}{2LC}.$$

(b) Resonance ($Z$ real): $\omega L=\frac1{\omega C}$, so $\omega=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$.

Show solution 10 — R, L, C all in parallel

$\frac1Z=\frac1R+\frac1{i\omega L}+i\omega C=\frac1R+i\left(\omega C-\frac1{\omega L}\right)$.

(a) $\arg Z=-\arg\frac1Z=45^\circ$ means $\omega C-\frac1{\omega L}=-\frac1R$, i.e. $LC\omega^2+\frac LR\omega-1=0$, so

$$\omega=\frac{-\frac LR+\sqrt{\frac{L^2}{R^2}+4LC}}{2LC}.$$

(If instead $\arg Z=-45^\circ$, change the sign of the $\frac LR$ term.) (b) Resonance: $\omega C=\frac1{\omega L}$, so again $\omega=\frac1{\sqrt{LC}}$. There $Z=R$ is a maximum (the current drawn is smallest), the opposite of the series case.

Show solution 11 — sums of odd multiples

$\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{i(2k-1)\theta}=e^{i\theta}\frac{1-e^{2in\theta}}{1-e^{2i\theta}}=e^{i\theta}\frac{e^{in\theta}(-2i\sin n\theta)}{e^{i\theta}(-2i\sin\theta)}=e^{in\theta}\frac{\sin n\theta}{\sin\theta}.$

Real part: $\dfrac{\cos n\theta\sin n\theta}{\sin\theta}=\dfrac{\sin2n\theta}{2\sin\theta}$ ✔. Imaginary part: $\dfrac{\sin^2n\theta}{\sin\theta}$ ✔.

Show solution 12 — multiple reflections in a film

$\left(\sum r^{2n}\cos n\theta\right)^2+\left(\sum r^{2n}\sin n\theta\right)^2=|X|^2+|Y|^2$ with $X+iY=\sum_{n=0}^\infty r^{2n}e^{in\theta}$, which is $|X+iY|^2$ since $X$ and $Y$ are real.

It is a geometric series with ratio $r^2e^{i\theta}$, $|r|\lt1$: $\frac{1}{1-r^2e^{i\theta}}$. So the expression equals

$$\frac{1}{|1-r^2e^{i\theta}|^2}=\frac{1}{1-2r^2\cos\theta+r^4}.$$

(This is the Airy function of a Fabry–Pérot interferometer.)

Show solution 13 — SHM solutions

$\frac{d^2}{dt^2}e^{\pm i\omega t}=(\pm i\omega)^2e^{\pm i\omega t}=-\omega^2e^{\pm i\omega t}$. Also $\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\cos\omega t=-\omega^2\cos\omega t$ and $\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\sin\omega t=-\omega^2\sin\omega t$. All four satisfy $\ddot y=-\omega^2y$.

6. More practice

P1. A series circuit has $R=3\,\Omega$, $\omega L=8\,\Omega$, $\frac{1}{\omega C}=4\,\Omega$, and $V_0=10$ V. Find $Z$, $|Z|$, the phase, and $I_0$. Easy
Show solution

$Z=3+4i$, $|Z|=5\,\Omega$, phase $\arctan\frac43=53.1^\circ$ (voltage leads), $I_0=10/5=2$ A.

P2. Find the average power $\frac12\operatorname{Re}(V\bar I)$ in P1. Medium
Show solution

$V=ZI$, so $V\bar I=Z|I|^2$ and $P=\frac12|I|^2\operatorname{Re}Z=\frac12\cdot4\cdot3=6$ W. Only the resistor dissipates power.

P3. Find the angles $\delta$ where the 4-slit intensity $\frac{\sin^2(2\delta)}{\sin^2(\delta/2)}$ is zero, for $0\lt\delta\lt2\pi$. Medium
Show solution

$\sin2\delta=0$ with $\sin\frac\delta2\ne0$: $\delta=\frac\pi2,\pi,\frac{3\pi}2$. That is 3 zeros between principal maxima, and $n-2=2$ subsidiary maxima between them.

P4. A damped oscillator $\ddot y+2\gamma\dot y+\omega_0^2y=0$. Try $y=e^{st}$ and find $s$ for $\gamma\lt\omega_0$. Medium
Show solution

$s^2+2\gamma s+\omega_0^2=0$, so $s=-\gamma\pm i\sqrt{\omega_0^2-\gamma^2}$. Hence $y=e^{-\gamma t}\left(Ae^{i\omega_1t}+Be^{-i\omega_1t}\right)$ with $\omega_1=\sqrt{\omega_0^2-\gamma^2}$: a decaying oscillation, i.e. a spiral $z(t)$ in the complex plane.

P5. Show $\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\cos(k\delta)=\dfrac{\sin(n\delta/2)}{\sin(\delta/2)}\cos\dfrac{(n-1)\delta}{2}$. Medium
Show solution

Take the real part of (16.20) with $\omega t=0$.

P6. (NET) At resonance in a series LCR circuit, the phase difference between current and voltage is: Easy
Show solution

Zero: $Z=R$ is real.

7. Summary

  • Motion: $v=|\dot z|$, $a=|\ddot z|$. Multiplying by $i$ turns by $90^\circ$, which gives the centripetal acceleration $v^2/a$.
  • a.c.: $I=I_0e^{i\omega t}$, $V=ZI$, $Z=R+i(\omega L-\frac1{\omega C})$. Combine impedances like resistors. Resonance means $Z$ is real.
  • Optics: a geometric series of phasors gives $\frac{\sin(n\delta/2)}{\sin(\delta/2)}$, and the intensity is the grating pattern.
  • SHM: $y=\operatorname{Re}(y_0e^{i\omega t})$, with $|y_0|$ the amplitude and $\arg y_0$ the phase.
