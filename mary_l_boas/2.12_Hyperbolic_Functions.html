2.12 Hyperbolic Functions

Boas, Chapter 2, Section 12 · $\sinh$, $\cosh$, $\tanh$ and friends, their link to $\sin$ and $\cos$ of imaginary arguments, all the identities, and why they are called "hyperbolic".

Goals

  • Move between trig and hyperbolic functions with $\sin iz=i\sinh z$, $\cos iz=\cosh z$.
  • Derive any hyperbolic identity (Osborn's rule) and the real/imaginary-part formulas for $\sin z$, $\cos z$, $\sinh z$, $\cosh z$.
  • Understand the hyperbola picture (Boas Problem 38) and where these functions appear in physics.

1. Where they come from

Put $z=iy$ (pure imaginary) into the definitions of $\sin z$ and $\cos z$ (11.4):

$$\sin iy=\frac{e^{-y}-e^{y}}{2i}=i\,\frac{e^y-e^{-y}}{2},\qquad\cos iy=\frac{e^{-y}+e^{y}}{2}.\qquad(12.1)$$

These real combinations of exponentials appear so often that they get names:

Definitions (12.2), (12.3) $$\sinh z=\frac{e^z-e^{-z}}{2},\qquad\cosh z=\frac{e^z+e^{-z}}{2},$$ $$\tanh z=\frac{\sinh z}{\cosh z},\quad\coth z=\frac{1}{\tanh z},\quad\operatorname{sech}z=\frac1{\cosh z},\quad\operatorname{csch}z=\frac1{\sinh z}.$$

Read "sinh" as "shine" or "sinch", and "cosh" as written.

Then (12.1) says:

$$\boxed{\sin iz=i\sinh z,\qquad\cos iz=\cosh z}\qquad(12.4)$$

and also $\sinh iz=i\sin z$, $\cosh iz=\cos z$, $\tan iz=i\tanh z$, $\tanh iz=i\tan z$ (Problems 14–17). Hyperbolic functions are trig functions rotated by $90^\circ$ in the complex plane, up to a factor $i$.

3. Why "hyperbolic"? (Boas Problem 38)

$x=\cos t,\ y=\sin t$ satisfy $x^2+y^2=1$: a circle. $\ x=\cosh t,\ y=\sinh t$ satisfy

$$\cosh^2t-\sinh^2t=\frac{(e^t+e^{-t})^2-(e^t-e^{-t})^2}{4}=\frac{4}{4}=1,$$

so the point lies on the hyperbola $x^2-y^2=1$ (right branch, since $\cosh t\ge1$).

The deep parallel: $t$ is twice an area

For the circle, the shaded sector from $(1,0)$ to $(\cos t,\sin t)$ has area $\frac t2$. For the hyperbola, the region between the $x$-axis, the hyperbola, and the line from the origin to $(\cosh t,\sinh t)$ also has area exactly $\frac t2$. So $t$ plays the same role for both curves, and $\sinh$ and $\cosh$ are "sine and cosine for the hyperbola".

Interactive 2 — Circle and hyperbola side by side
Move $t$. On the circle the point is $(\cos t,\sin t)$, and on the hyperbola it is $(\cosh t,\sinh t)$. Both shaded areas equal $t/2$. The circle point goes round and round (period $2\pi$), but the hyperbola point runs off to infinity: real hyperbolic functions are not periodic. Their period $2\pi i$ is imaginary.

4. Identities — and Osborn's rule

Because of (12.4), every trig identity becomes a hyperbolic identity, but some signs change. Example (Boas): $\cosh^2z-\sinh^2z=1$ (compare $\sin^2z+\cos^2z=1$), and $\frac{d}{dz}\cosh z=+\sinh z$ (compare $\frac{d}{dz}\cos z=-\sin z$).

Osborn's rule

To turn a trig identity into a hyperbolic one: replace $\cos\to\cosh$ and $\sin\to\sinh$, and change the sign of every term that contains a product of two sines (including $\tan^2$, $\sin^2$, $\sin a\sin b$, …).

Why it works: put $z\to iz$ in the trig identity. Each $\sin$ becomes $i\sinh$, so a product of two sines gets $i^2=-1$.

The rule is for algebraic identities. Derivatives have their own signs: $(\sinh z)'=\cosh z$, $(\cosh z)'=\sinh z$ (no minus!), $(\tanh z)'=\operatorname{sech}^2z$.

Real and imaginary parts (Boas Problems 1–4)

Using the addition formulas and (12.4), e.g. $\sin(x+iy)=\sin x\cos iy+\cos x\sin iy$:

$$\begin{aligned}\sin z&=\sin x\cosh y+i\cos x\sinh y, &\cos z&=\cos x\cosh y-i\sin x\sinh y,\\ \sinh z&=\sinh x\cos y+i\cosh x\sin y, &\cosh z&=\cosh x\cos y+i\sinh x\sin y.\end{aligned}$$

Taking $|\ |^2$ and using $\cosh^2=1+\sinh^2$:

$$|\sin z|^2=\sin^2x+\sinh^2y,\quad|\cos z|^2=\cos^2x+\sinh^2y,\quad|\sinh z|^2=\sinh^2x+\sin^2y,\quad|\cosh z|^2=\sinh^2x+\cos^2y .$$

Periods and zeros in the complex plane

  • $\sinh$ and $\cosh$ have period $2\pi i$, and $\tanh$ has period $\pi i$ (because $\sinh(z+i\pi)=-\sinh z$ and $\cosh(z+i\pi)=-\cosh z$).
  • $\sinh z=0\iff z=n\pi i$, and $\cosh z=0\iff z=\left(n+\frac12\right)\pi i$. (Compare the zeros of $\sin$ and $\cos$, rotated by $90^\circ$.)

5. Where hyperbolic functions appear in physics

  • Hanging chain (catenary): $y=a\cosh(x/a)$. The arc length from $0$ to $x$ is $a\sinh(x/a)$, since $\sqrt{1+\sinh^2}=\cosh$.
  • Special relativity: rapidity $\phi$ with $v/c=\tanh\phi$. The Lorentz boost is a "hyperbolic rotation": $ct'=ct\cosh\phi-x\sinh\phi$, $x'=x\cosh\phi-ct\sinh\phi$. Velocity addition $\frac{u+v}{1+uv/c^2}$ is just $\tanh(\phi_1+\phi_2)$.
  • Overdamped oscillator, heat conduction, transmission lines: solutions of $y''=k^2y$ are $A\cosh kx+B\sinh kx$.
  • Quantum tunnelling: inside a barrier the wave function is $\cosh$ and $\sinh$ of $\kappa x$, and transmission probabilities contain $\sinh^2(\kappa a)$ (see Boas Problem 17.28).
  • Statistical mechanics: the magnetisation of a paramagnet is $\tanh(\mu B/kT)$.

7. More practice

P1. Compute $\sinh(\ln3)$, $\cosh(\ln3)$, $\tanh(\ln3)$. Easy
Show solution

$\frac{3-\frac13}{2}=\frac43$, $\ \frac{3+\frac13}2=\frac53$, $\ \frac45$. In general $\tanh(\ln a)=\frac{a^2-1}{a^2+1}$.

P2. Solve $\cosh z=0$ and $\sinh z=i$. Medium
Show solution

$\cosh z=\cos(iz)=0$ gives $iz=\frac\pi2+n\pi$, so $z=-i\left(\frac\pi2+n\pi\right)$, i.e. $z=\left(n+\frac12\right)\pi i$.

$\sinh z=-i\sin(iz)=i$ gives $\sin(iz)=-1$, so $iz=-\frac\pi2+2n\pi$, i.e. $z=i\left(\frac\pi2-2n\pi\right)$. All solutions are $z=\left(\frac\pi2+2k\pi\right)i$.

P3. Show $\frac{d}{dz}\tanh z=\operatorname{sech}^2z$ and $\int\operatorname{sech}^2z\,dz=\tanh z$. Easy
Show solution

Quotient rule: $\frac{\cosh^2z-\sinh^2z}{\cosh^2z}=\frac{1}{\cosh^2z}$. The integral follows directly.

P4. Using rapidities, show that relativistic velocity addition is $\frac{u+v}{1+uv/c^2}$. Medium
Show solution

Put $u/c=\tanh a$ and $v/c=\tanh b$. Rapidities add under successive boosts, so $w/c=\tanh(a+b)=\frac{\tanh a+\tanh b}{1+\tanh a\tanh b}=\frac{u/c+v/c}{1+uv/c^2}$. Since $|\tanh|\lt1$, the result can never exceed $c$.

P5. The catenary $y=a\cosh\frac xa$: find the length of the chain from $x=-L$ to $x=L$. Medium
Show solution

$\int_{-L}^{L}\sqrt{1+\sinh^2\frac xa}\,dx=\int_{-L}^L\cosh\frac xa\,dx=2a\sinh\frac La$.

P6. Find $|\sinh(1+i)|$ and $\operatorname{Re}\cosh(1+i)$. Easy
Show solution

$|\sinh(1+i)|^2=\sinh^21+\sin^21=1.3811+0.7081=2.0892$, so $|\sinh(1+i)|\approx1.4454$. $\operatorname{Re}\cosh(1+i)=\cosh1\cos1\approx0.8337$.

P7. Prove $\cosh(a+b)=\cosh a\cosh b+\sinh a\sinh b$ directly from exponentials. Medium
Show solution

$\frac14[(e^a+e^{-a})(e^b+e^{-b})+(e^a-e^{-a})(e^b-e^{-b})]=\frac14[2e^{a+b}+2e^{-a-b}]=\cosh(a+b)$. The cross terms cancel.

P8. (NET) The general solution of $y''-k^2y=0$ with $y(0)=1$, $y'(0)=0$ is: Easy
Show solution

$y=A\cosh kx+B\sinh kx$. The conditions give $A=1$ and $B=0$, so $y=\cosh kx$.

9. Summary

  • $\sinh z=\frac{e^z-e^{-z}}2$ and $\cosh z=\frac{e^z+e^{-z}}2$, with $e^z=\cosh z+\sinh z$.
  • $\sin iz=i\sinh z$, $\cos iz=\cosh z$, $\sinh iz=i\sin z$, $\cosh iz=\cos z$.
  • Osborn's rule: flip the sign of products of two sines. Also $(\cosh)'=\sinh$ (no minus).
  • $\sinh(x+iy)=\sinh x\cos y+i\cosh x\sin y$, $\ \cosh(x+iy)=\cosh x\cos y+i\sinh x\sin y$.
  • $(\cosh t,\sinh t)$ lies on $x^2-y^2=1$, and $t$ is twice the sector area.
