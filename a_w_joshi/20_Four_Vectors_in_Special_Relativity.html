0. Roadmap

Section 15 argued that physical laws must be tensor equations, so that they look the same to every observer. In Newtonian physics "every observer" meant observers with rotated or shifted axes (Galilean invariance). Special relativity adds observers moving uniformly relative to each other. Then:

  • space intervals dx and time intervals dt are no longer separately invariant, but the interval ds² = c²dt² − dx² is;
  • so the "vectors" of physics become four-vectors in a 4D space-time (Minkowski space) with metric diag(1, −1, −1, −1);
  • a Lorentz transformation plays the role that rotations played in Section 19.
ds² invariant→metric g = diag(1,−1,−1,−1)→Lorentz boost (γ, β)→four-vectors Ai = (A⁰, A)→invariant A⁰B⁰ − A·B→∂i, □
Needed from earlier Contravariant vs covariant components and the transformation law (Section 15), the metric tensor, raising and lowering indices (Section 18), the gradient being covariant (Example 15.3b), rotations and Cartesian tensors (Section 19), hyperbolic functions cosh, sinh, tanh.

1. The invariant interval (Eq. 1)

Two events, e.g. "a child is born here" and "a star explodes there", are separated by a space interval dx and a time interval dt. Observers in relative motion measure different dx and dt. What they all agree on is

(ds)² = c²(dt)² − [(dx¹)² + (dx²)² + (dx³)²]   (1)

where c is the speed of light. Transformations that keep ds² unchanged between inertial observers are the Lorentz transformations. They include uniform relative motion as well as rotations and translations.

Sign of ds²NameMeaning
ds² > 0timelikeone event can cause the other; a clock can travel between them. ds/c = proper time.
ds² = 0lightlike (null)connected by a light signal
ds² < 0spacelikeno signal can connect them; their time order depends on the observer

Since the sign of ds² is invariant, these three classes are the same for every observer: cause and effect are never reversed.

2. Four-vectors (Eqs. 2–9)

A four-vector (world vector) is an ordered set of 4 components that transforms like (ct, x) under Lorentz transformations. Each one is a Galilean scalar (time part) plus a Galilean vector (space part). Its "length" (A⁰)² − |A|² is invariant.

Four-vectorComponents Ai = (A⁰, A)Invariant (A⁰)² − |A|²
(a) position / intervalxi = (ct, x), x⁰ = ctc²t² − |x|² (= s²)
(b) four-velocityUi = (γc, γv), γ = (1 − v²/c²)−1/2  (2, 3)γ²c² − γ²v² = c²  (4)
(c) four-momentumpi = (E/c, p), E = γm₀c², p = γm₀v  (5)m₀²c²  (6), i.e. E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴
(d) four-currentJi = (cρ, J)  (7)c²ρ₀² (ρ₀ = rest-frame density)
(e) four-potentialAi = (Φ, A)  (8)—
(f) four-wave vectorki = (ω/c, k)  (9)0 for light in vacuum (ω = c|k|)
Why Ui = γ(c, v)? Ui = dxi/dτ, where dτ = ds/c = dt/γ is the proper time, which is invariant. dxi is a four-vector and dτ a scalar, so Ui is a four-vector. Then pi = m₀Ui follows at once. (Units: the book writes A⁰ = Φ, which is the convention with c absorbed in the units; in SI it is Φ/c.)

3. Galilean scalars vs world scalars

  • Galilean scalars (invariant under rotations only): mass, energy, length, time interval, volume, charge density. These are not world scalars: a moving rod is shorter, a moving clock is slower, a moving charge distribution is denser.
  • World scalars (Lorentz invariant): c, electric charge q, rest mass m₀, proper time τ, ds², and any full contraction AiBi.
  • Charge q is a world scalar, but charge density ρ = q/V is not, because volume contracts. Instead cρ is the time part of the four-vector Ji.

4. Lorentz transformation along x¹ (Eqs. 10–12)

Frame S′ moves with velocity v along x¹ relative to S, axes parallel. With β = v/c (0 ≤ β < 1) and γ = 1/√(1 − β²):

x̄⁰ = γ(x⁰ − βx¹)
x̄¹ = γ(x¹ − βx⁰)
x̄² = x²,   x̄³ = x³   (10)     inverse: β → −β  (12)

As a matrix, Λ = [[γ, −γβ], [−γβ, γ]] acting on (x⁰, x¹). Write β = tanh ψ (ψ = rapidity). Then γ = cosh ψ, γβ = sinh ψ, and

Λ(ψ) =
cosh ψ−sinh ψ
−sinh ψcosh ψ

It is a "rotation" by an imaginary angle. cosh² − sinh² = 1 is why (x⁰)² − (x¹)² is unchanged, exactly as cos² + sin² = 1 keeps x² + y² unchanged under a rotation. Boosts along the same line add their rapidities: ψ = ψ₁ + ψ₂, which gives the velocity-addition law β = (β₁ + β₂)/(1 + β₁β₂).

What (10) says physically

  • Time dilation: a clock at rest in S′ (x̄¹ fixed) gives dx̄⁰ = dx⁰/γ: moving clocks run slow.
  • Length contraction: a rod at rest in S′ of length L₀ is measured in S (both ends at the same x⁰) as L₀/γ.
  • Relativity of simultaneity: two events with the same x⁰ but different x¹ have x̄⁰ differing by −γβ Δx¹: the "time lag" γβx¹.
  • c is the limit: γ is real only for β < 1.

6. Boost in an arbitrary direction (Eqs. 13–23)

Let β = v/c point in any direction (axes of S and S′ still parallel). Split the position into parts parallel and perpendicular to β (Fig. 16): x = x∥ + x⊥.  (15)

  1. Time: the dilation factor γ depends only on |β|. The time lag γβx¹ in (10) must become γ(β₁x¹ + β₂x² + β₃x³), the only expression that reduces correctly when β lies along any axis:
    x̄⁰ = γ(x⁰ − β·x)   (14)
  2. Perpendicular part: unchanged, x̄⊥ = x⊥.  (17)
  3. Parallel part: transforms like x¹ in (10): x̄∥ = γ(x∥ − βx⁰).  (18)
  4. Add them: x̄ = γx∥ + x⊥ − γβx⁰ = x + (γ − 1)x∥ − γβx⁰.  (19)
  5. With x∥ = (x·β)β/β² (20, 21):
x̄⁰ = γ(x⁰ − β·x),    x̄ = x + [(γ − 1)/β²](x·β)β − γβx⁰   (23)

Every four-vector transforms the same way (24):

Ā⁰ = γ(A⁰ − β·A),   Ā∥ = γ(A∥ − βA⁰),   Ā⊥ = A⊥,   i.e. Ā = A + [(γ−1)/β²](A·β)β − γβA⁰  (24a–d)

As a 4×4 matrix: Λ⁰₀ = γ, Λ⁰j = Λj₀ = −γβj, Λij = δij + (γ − 1)βiβj/β². It is symmetric.

Two boosts do not make a boost A boost along x followed by a boost along y is not a pure boost. The product matrix is not symmetric: it is a boost times a rotation (the Wigner rotation). This causes Thomas precession, which halves the spin–orbit coupling in atoms. The boost lab shows the non-symmetric product.

8. The invariant scalar product (Example 1, Eqs. 25–30)

Example 1 For four-vectors (A⁰, A), (B⁰, B), the scalar product A⁰B⁰ − A·B is Lorentz invariant.
  1. Write Ā⁰B̄⁰ − Ā·B̄ = Ā⁰B̄⁰ − Ā∥B̄∥ − Ā⊥·B̄⊥.  (26)
  2. Insert (24): = γ²(A⁰ − βA∥)(B⁰ − βB∥) − γ²(A∥ − βA⁰)(B∥ − βB⁰) − A⊥·B⊥, using β·A = βA∥.
  3. Expand: the cross terms −γ²β(A⁰B∥ + A∥B⁰) cancel between the two products, leaving γ²(1 − β²)A⁰B⁰ − γ²(1 − β²)A∥B∥ − A⊥·B⊥.  (27)
  4. γ²(1 − β²) = 1 (28), so the result is A⁰B⁰ − A∥B∥ − A⊥·B⊥ = A⁰B⁰ − A·B. ∎  (29)

Special case A = B: (Ā⁰)² − |Ā|² = (A⁰)² − |A|².  (30)   Example: pipi = m₀²c² for every observer.

9. Minkowski metric, covariant components and the four-gradient (Eqs. 31–42)

Comparing (1) with ds² = gijdxidxj (18.1):

[gij] = diag(1, −1, −1, −1)  (31),    gij = gij  (32)

(It is its own inverse.) A contravariant four-vector transforms as Āα = (∂x̄α/∂xi)Ai (33). Lowering the index flips the sign of the space part:

A0 = A⁰,   Ak = −Ak  (34);    Ai = (A⁰, A),   Ai = (A⁰, −A)  (35);    A·B ≡ AiBi = A⁰B⁰ − A·B  (36)

So the invariant of Example 1 is simply the contraction AiBi, a scalar by Section 16. Here the difference between upper and lower components does not vanish, even though the coordinates are "Cartesian", because gij ≠ δij.

Four-gradient

By the chain rule ∂/∂x̄α = (∂xi/∂x̄α)∂/∂xi (37), so ∂/∂xi is a covariant vector operator:

∂i ≡ ∂/∂xi = ((1/c)∂/∂t, ∇)  (38, 39a),    ∂i ≡ ∂/∂xi = ((1/c)∂/∂t, −∇)  (39b),   xi = gijxj  (40)
  • Four-gradient of a scalar: ∂iφ (covariant) or ∂iφ.
  • Four-divergence: ∂iAi = ∂iAi = ∂A⁰/∂x⁰ + ∇·A, a Lorentz scalar.  (41)   (The book prints "− ∇·A" in (41). With Ai = (A⁰, A) and ∂i = (∂₀, ∇), the sign is +, which is what makes ∂iJi = 0 the continuity equation in Section 21.)
  • d'Alembertian: □ = ∂i∂i = (1/c²)∂²/∂t² − ∇²  (42), the operator of the wave equation □φ = 0, invariant under Lorentz transformations.

12. Book's Exercise 20: solved

1. Consider x⁰ = r cosh ψ, x¹ = r sin θ cos φ sinh ψ, x² = r sin θ sin φ sinh ψ, x³ = r cos θ sinh ψ. (a) Using the metric (1), obtain the metric in terms of (r, θ, φ, ψ). (b) Obtain the inverse transformation.
Show solution (a)

Write R = |x| = r sinh ψ. The spatial part is ordinary spherical coordinates with radius R: |dx|² = dR² + R²(dθ² + sin²θ dφ²).

dx⁰ = cosh ψ dr + r sinh ψ dψ,   dR = sinh ψ dr + r cosh ψ dψ.

(dx⁰)² − dR² = dr²(cosh²ψ − sinh²ψ) + r²dψ²(sinh²ψ − cosh²ψ) + 2r dr dψ(cosh ψ sinh ψ − sinh ψ cosh ψ) = dr² − r²dψ².

ds² = dr² − r²[dψ² + sinh²ψ (dθ² + sin²θ dφ²)]

gij = diag(1, −r², −r²sinh²ψ, −r²sinh²ψ sin²θ) in the order (r, ψ, θ, φ). Diagonal, so the coordinates are orthogonal. The bracket is the metric of 3D hyperbolic space: each surface r = const is a hyperboloid c²t² − |x|² = r², all of whose points are reached from the origin by observers moving at different velocities. ψ is their rapidity.

Show solution (b)

(x⁰)² − |x|² = r²(cosh²ψ − sinh²ψ) = r², so

r = √[(x⁰)² − (x¹)² − (x²)² − (x³)²],   tanh ψ = √[(x¹)² + (x²)² + (x³)²]/x⁰,
θ = tan⁻¹[√((x¹)² + (x²)²)/x³],   φ = tan⁻¹(x²/x¹)

These coordinates cover only the region inside the future light cone (x⁰ > |x|, timelike from the origin), because cosh ψ > sinh ψ ≥ 0. Note tanh ψ = |x|/x⁰ = velocity/c of an observer who starts at the origin and reaches the event.

13. Practice set (CSIR-NET / GATE style)

P1. A particle has E = 5 GeV and |p|c = 4 GeV. Find its rest energy and speed. invariant
Show solution

m₀c² = √(25 − 16) = 3 GeV. β = pc/E = 0.8, γ = 5/3.

P2. A muon (τ₀ = 2.2 μs) moves with γ = 20. How far does it travel in one mean lifetime? dilation
Show solution

βγcτ₀ = √(400 − 1)·(3×10⁸)(2.2×10⁻⁶) ≈ 19.97 × 660 m ≈ 13.2 km. Without time dilation it would only be about 660 m.

P3. Two rockets approach each other, each at 0.8c relative to Earth. Speed of one relative to the other? addition
Show solution

(0.8 + 0.8)/(1 + 0.64) = 1.6/1.64 ≈ 0.976c. Rapidities: tanh⁻¹0.8 ≈ 1.099, doubled = 2.197, tanh(2.197) ≈ 0.976 ✓.

P4. Events (ct, x) = (0, 0) and (3, 5) m. Classify the interval. Is there a frame where they are simultaneous? interval
Show solution

s² = 9 − 25 = −16 < 0: spacelike. Yes: need βx¹ = x⁰, β = 3/5. In that frame they are 4 m apart (proper distance).

P5. A source emits light of frequency ω. What frequency does an observer moving directly toward it at β = 0.6 see? Doppler
Show solution

Approaching: ω̄ = ω√((1 + β)/(1 − β)) = ω√(1.6/0.4) = 2ω.

P6. Show ∂ixi = 4 and ∂ixj = δij. four-gradient
Show solution

∂xj/∂xi = δji; contracting gives δii = 4 (the dimension of space-time).

P7. Show Uiai = 0 where ai = dUi/dτ. four-acceleration
Show solution

UiUi = c² is constant; differentiate with respect to τ: 2Uiai = 0. The four-acceleration is always orthogonal (in the Minkowski sense) to the four-velocity.

P8. Find the threshold photon energy for γ + p → p + π⁰ on a proton at rest (mpc² = 938 MeV, mπc² = 135 MeV). invariant mass
Show solution

Invariant s = (Eγ + mp)² − Eγ² = mp² + 2Eγmp must reach (mp + mπ)². Eγ = [(mp + mπ)² − mp²]/(2mp) = (1073² − 938²)/1876 ≈ 145 MeV.

15. Interesting facts

Minkowski 1908 Hermann Minkowski (Einstein's former maths teacher) announced: "Henceforth space by itself, and time by itself, are doomed to fade away into mere shadows, and only a kind of union of the two will preserve an independent reality." Einstein at first called the four-dimensional formalism "superfluous learnedness". He changed his mind when he needed it for general relativity.
Rapidity in colliders Particle physicists at the LHC describe particles by rapidity rather than velocity, because rapidities simply add under boosts along the beam. The shape of a distribution in rapidity is the same in every frame moving along the beam.
Thomas precession Llewellyn Thomas (1926) showed that the non-commuting of boosts (Section 6) gives an extra rotation of the electron's spin frame, which accounts for a missing factor of ½ in the atomic fine structure. At the time it rescued the new idea of electron spin.
Sign conventions This book (and most particle physics) uses (+, −, −, −). Many GR and string theory texts use (−, +, +, +). The physics is identical; only the signs of pipi and □ flip. Always check the convention before comparing formulas.
