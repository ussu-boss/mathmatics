0. Roadmap

Section 20 found four-vectors such as Ji = (cρ, J) and Ai = (Φ, A). But E and B are not parts of any four-vector. They mix into each other when you change frame. A charge at rest has only E. Seen by a moving observer, the same charge is a current, so B appears too.

The way out: the six components of E and B together form one antisymmetric rank-2 tensor Fij in space-time (4·3/2 = 6 components). Then all of Maxwell's equations collapse into two tensor equations, which are automatically the same in every inertial frame.

Ai = (Φ, A)→Fij = ∂iAj − ∂jAi→∂iFij = (4π/c)Jj, ∂i𝔉ij = 0→F̄ = ΛFΛᵀ→E² − B², E·B invariant
Needed from earlier Four-vectors, Lorentz boost, metric diag(1, −1, −1, −1), ∂i = (∂₀, ∇), ∂i = (∂₀, −∇), □ = ∂i∂i (Section 20); antisymmetric tensors and ε (Section 16); raising and lowering with g (Section 18). Units: Gaussian (∇·E = 4πρ), as in the book. In SI replace 4π/c by μ₀ and E by E/c where needed.

3. Maxwell's equations in tensor form (Eqs. 19–36)

∇·E = 4πρ  (19a)inhomogeneous (sources)∂iFij = (4π/c)Jj  (24)

How the pieces come out

  • j = 0 in (24): ∂₁F¹⁰ + ∂₂F²⁰ + ∂₃F³⁰ = ∂xEx + ∂yEy + ∂zEz = (4π/c)(cρ). That is Gauss's law (19a), using F⁰⁰ = 0.  (20, 21)
  • j = 1 in (24): ∂₂F²¹ + ∂₃F³¹ + ∂₀F⁰¹ = ∂yBz − ∂zBy − (1/c)∂tEx = (4π/c)Jx, the x-part of the Ampère–Maxwell law (19b).  (22, 23)
  • Homogeneous, j = 0: ∂i𝔉i0 = ∇·B = 0.  (27)   j = 1: ∂i𝔉i1 = 0 is the x-part of Faraday's law (19d).  (28–30)
  • Cyclic form (34): with the covariant ∂i, the indices in (29) do not appear in cyclic order. Using ∂i instead (32, 33) gives the neat cyclic identity (34), which is also automatic: if Fij = ∂iAj − ∂jAi, the six terms cancel in pairs because mixed partials commute.
Charge conservation is built in Apply ∂j to (24): ∂j∂iFij = 0, because ∂j∂i is symmetric and Fij antisymmetric (Exercise 16.7 idea). So ∂jJj = 0: Maxwell's equations force the continuity equation (4).

4. Transformation of E and B (Eqs. 37–54)

Fij is a rank-2 contravariant tensor: F̄αβ = (∂x̄α/∂xi)(∂x̄β/∂xj)Fij (37). With the boost matrix A = [aαi] = [∂x̄α/∂xi] (38, 39), which is symmetric for a boost along x¹:

F̄ = A F Ã   (40)

Multiplying out and comparing with the form (13) for F̄ (41, 42) gives, for β along x¹:

Ēx = Ex,     B̄x = Bx
Ēy = γ(Ey − βBz),   B̄y = γ(By + βEz)
Ēz = γ(Ez + βBy),   B̄z = γ(Bz − βEy)   (43a)    inverse: β → −β (43b)

Any direction of β (Eqs. 44–54)

  1. Components parallel to β do not change: Ē∥ = E∥.  (46)
  2. Perpendicular part: Ē⊥ = γ[E⊥ + β(Byk − Bzj)], and i × B = Byk − Bzj, so Ē⊥ = γ(E⊥ + β × B).  (47–49)
  3. Combine with E∥ = (E·β)β/β² (51):
Ē = γ(E + β × B) − [γ²/(γ + 1)] β(β·E),    B̄ = γ(B − β × E) − [γ²/(γ + 1)] β(β·B)   (54a, b)

using β²γ² = γ² − 1 (53), so (γ − 1)/β² = γ²/(γ + 1). As β → 0, Ē = E and B̄ = B: E and B are invariant only for observers at rest relative to each other.

Physical reading Ē = γ(E + β × B) for perpendicular parts is the relativistic version of the Lorentz force law: a charge moving with v in fields E, B feels the force q(E + v × B/c), and in its rest frame that whole force must be electric.

6. Invariants of the field (Example 1, Eqs. 55–58)

Example 1 (a) E·B and (b) E² − B² are Lorentz invariant.

First proof (direct): with (43a), Ē·B̄ = ExBx + γ²[EyBy + β(EyEz − BzBy) − β²EzBz] + γ²[EzBz + β(ByBz − EyEz) − β²EyBy]. The β terms cancel and γ²(1 − β²) = 1, leaving E·B.  (55)   Similarly Ē² − B̄² = E² − B².  (56)

Second proof (contractions are scalars):

FijFij = 2(B² − E²)  (57),    Fij𝔉ij = −4E·B  (58)

Both are full contractions, hence world scalars (Section 16). Checked symbolically.

Consequences (Exercise 3 and beyond)

SituationCan you find a frame with…
E·B ≠ 0neither field zero in any frame. Best possible: E̅ ∥ B̅.
E·B = 0, E > BB̄ = 0 (pure electric), with β = E × B/E²
E·B = 0, B > EĒ = 0 (pure magnetic), with β = E × B/B²
E·B = 0, E = Bneither: a radiation field (plane wave) looks like a wave in every frame

The sign of E² − B² cannot change, so "mostly electric" stays mostly electric for every observer.

7. Field of a uniformly moving charge (Eqs. 59–66)

Charge q moves with velocity v along x¹ in S. Find the field at P = (0, d, 0), where d is the distance of closest approach (Fig. 17). Trick: go to the charge's rest frame S′, where there is only a Coulomb field, and transform back.

  1. In S′ the charge is at the origin and P is at x̄¹ = −vt̄, x̄² = d, x̄³ = 0. Distance r̄ = (d² + v²t̄²)1/2.  (59, 60)
  2. At P, x¹ = 0, so by (20.10) t̄ = γ(t − vx¹/c²) = γt. Hence r̄ = (d² + γ²v²t²)1/2.  (61, 62)
  3. Coulomb field in S′: Ēx = −qvt̄/r̄³, Ēy = qd/r̄³, B̄ = 0.  (63)
  4. Express with t (64), then transform back with (43b), using B̄ = 0: Ex = Ēx, Ey = γĒy, Bz = γβĒy.
Ex = −qγvt/(d² + γ²v²t²)3/2,   Ey = γqd/(d² + γ²v²t²)3/2,   Bz = βEy   (65)
  • At t = 0 (closest approach): Ex = 0, Ey = γq/d². The peak field is γ times the static value q/d².
  • Ex is odd in t and Ey is even (Fig. 18).
  • The pulse lasts about Δt = d/(γv) (66). Ex has its extremes at t = ±Δt/√2. As γ grows the pulse gets taller and shorter: the field lines crowd into a "pancake" perpendicular to the motion.
  • Bz = βEy: the moving charge is a current, so it makes a magnetic field. At low speed this is the Biot–Savart field qv × r/(cr³).

11. Book's Exercise 21: all problems solved

1. In frame S there is a uniform static E along x¹ and a uniform static B of magnitude 2E in the x¹x² plane at angle θ to the x¹ axis. Another frame moves relative to S such that the fields there are parallel. (a) Find |Ē| and |B̄| in terms of E and θ. (b) Find β if the relative velocity is along x³.
Show solution

(a) Use the invariants. E² − B² = E² − 4E² = −3E² and E·B = 2E² cos θ. In the new frame Ē ∥ B̄, so Ē·B̄ = ĒB̄ (taking 0 ≤ θ < 90°; otherwise antiparallel and use |cos θ|). Then

Ē² − B̄² = −3E²,   ĒB̄ = 2E²cos θ

Put x = Ē²: x − 4E⁴cos²θ/x = −3E² ⇒ x² + 3E²x − 4E⁴cos²θ = 0:

Ē² = (E²/2)[−3 + √(9 + 16cos²θ)],    B̄² = (E²/2)[3 + √(9 + 16cos²θ)]

Check θ = 0 (already parallel): Ē² = E²(−3 + 5)/2 = E² ✓, B̄² = 4E² ✓. Check θ = 90° (E ⊥ B, B > E): Ē = 0 and B̄ = √3 E, a pure magnetic field ✓.

(b) β = βx̂₃ is ⊥ to E and B, so β·E = β·B = 0 and Ē = γ(E + β × B), B̄ = γ(B − β × E). With E = E(1, 0, 0), B = 2E(cos θ, sin θ, 0):

Ē ∝ (1 − 2β sin θ, 2β cos θ, 0),    B̄ ∝ (2cos θ, 2sin θ − β, 0)

Parallel ⇔ the z-component of Ē × B̄ vanishes: (1 − 2β sin θ)(2 sin θ − β) − 4β cos²θ = 0 ⇒ 2β² sin θ − 5β + 2 sin θ = 0:

β = [5 − √(25 − 16 sin²θ)] / (4 sin θ)   (the root with β < 1)

This agrees with the general result β/(1 + β²) = |E × B|/(E² + B²) = 2 sin θ/5. Example θ = 60°: β = (5 − √13)/(2√3) ≈ 0.403. (Load "Exercise 21.1" in the field lab and press "Find frame".)

3. In some frame E > B. Can there be another frame with (a) only an electric field, (b) only a magnetic field?
Show solution

E² − B² > 0 is invariant. (b) A pure magnetic field needs E² − B² = −B̄² < 0. Impossible.

(a) A pure electric field needs both invariants to fit: E² − B² = Ē² > 0 ✓ and E·B = Ē·0 = 0. So it is possible if and only if E ⊥ B (E·B = 0). The frame moves with β = E × B/E² (then |β| = B/E < 1), which makes B̄ = γ(B − β × E) = 0. If E·B ≠ 0, no frame has only one field; the best is Ē ∥ B̄ (Exercise 1).

12. Practice set (CSIR-NET / GATE style)

P1. A frame has E = (0, 3, 0) and B = (0, 0, 5) (Gaussian). Is there a frame with E = 0? With which velocity? frames
Show solution

E·B = 0 and B > E ⇒ yes. β = E × B/B² = (15, 0, 0)/25 = (0.6, 0, 0). Then B̄ = 4 (invariant: −B̄² = 9 − 25).

P2. Find FijFij and Fij𝔉ij for a plane electromagnetic wave. invariants
Show solution

|E| = |B| and E ⊥ B ⇒ both invariants are zero. A light wave is "null" in every frame.

P3. How many independent components do Fij and the cyclic identity (34) have? count
Show solution

F: 6 (antisymmetric 4×4). (34) is nontrivial only for i, j, k all different: C(4,3) = 4 equations = ∇·B = 0 plus 3 components of Faraday's law.

P4. Show that ∂iFjk + ∂jFki + ∂kFij = 0 follows from F = ∂A − ∂A. identity
Show solution

Expand: ∂i∂jAk − ∂i∂kAj + ∂j∂kAi − ∂j∂iAk + ∂k∂iAj − ∂k∂jAi = 0, the terms cancelling in pairs.

P5. An electron passes a point at closest distance d with γ = 100. Compare the peak field and duration with the static case. moving charge
Show solution

Peak Ey = 100 × q/d²; duration ≈ d/(γv) ≈ d/(100c). Ultra-relativistic particles deliver short, intense "flat" field pulses (the Weizsäcker–Williams picture of a charge as a cloud of virtual photons).

P6. Show that the Lorentz gauge condition is Lorentz invariant. gauge
Show solution

∂iAi is the contraction of a covariant operator with a contravariant vector: a scalar. If it is zero in one frame it is zero in all.

P7. A long wire carries current I along z (neutral, so E = 0 in S). An observer moves along z with v. What fields does it see? transformation
Show solution

B is azimuthal (⊥ v). Ē = γ(E + β × B) = γβ × B, a radial electric field: the wire looks charged to the moving observer (length contraction affects the positive ions and the electrons differently). B̄ = γB.

14. Interesting facts

Einstein's starting point Einstein's 1905 paper opens with exactly the problem of this section: a magnet moving near a conductor and a conductor moving near a magnet give the same current, yet the classical explanations were completely different (an electric field in one case, a magnetic force in the other). The tensor Fij shows they are one phenomenon seen from two frames.
Magnetism as a relativistic effect Electrons in a wire drift at only about 0.1 mm/s, so their γ − 1 ≈ 10⁻²⁶. Yet the magnetic force between currents is easily felt, because it is the tiny relativistic correction applied to an enormous number of charges whose electric forces cancel exactly.
Electromagnetic duality The map E → B, B → −E that turns F into 𝔉 is a symmetry of the source-free Maxwell equations. Dirac (1931) showed that if magnetic monopoles existed, this duality would extend to sources and electric charge would have to be quantized. None have been found yet.
Differential forms In modern notation F is a 2-form, F = dA, and Maxwell's equations become dF = 0 and d⋆F = 4π⋆J. The homogeneous equation dF = 0 is just "d² = 0", which is the cyclic identity (34).
